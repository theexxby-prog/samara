About

Movement science,
applied to patient care.

My degree is in kinesiology: biomechanics, exercise physiology, motor control and musculoskeletal anatomy. What I like about it is how directly it maps onto a treatment room. When a patient struggles with a step-up or compensates on a squat, the coursework explains why, and the clinic shows what to do about it.

I got my first clinical hours as an EMT student intern at Nassau University Medical Center, rotating through the Trauma Bay, Pediatric Unit, ICU and Burn Unit. Before that I spent a summer with the head athletic trainer at Long Island University, taping, bracing and running recovery modalities for Division I football players. Most recently I worked as a physical therapy aide at Nee's Physical Therapy in Hoboken, guiding patients through their prescribed exercises under a PT's supervision.

The next step is a Doctor of Physical Therapy program. Until then I want to be in a clinic or hospital, learning from the people doing the work.
